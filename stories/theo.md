# Context

Theoriq is a team of 50+ people building infrastructure for AI agents in DeFi.
Their platform enables teams to orchestrate and govern multi-agent systems that operate with real assets.

By the time I joined, the technology started to emerge.
Agents could execute and generate workspace artifacts.

What didn't exist yet was a product teams and developers could trust.

---

# The Problem

As of beginning of 2025, AI agents weren't failing technically.
They were failing as products.

Teams couldn't:
- Observe what agents were doing in real time.
- Constrain risk in a meaningful way.
- Reason about behavior once real money was involved.

Without clarity, there was no trust.
Without trust, there was no adoption.

Agents remained experimental artifacts instead of shippable systems.

---

# The Tension

We needed to move fast in a space that rewards experimentation.
But we couldn't ship abstraction without responsibility.

Over-engineering governance would slow teams down.
Under-designing it would make agents unusable with real assets.

The challenge was finding a balance where:
- Systems felt powerful but constrained.
- Behavior felt observable, not opaque.
- Teams could ship without fear of losing control.

---

# My Role

I owned the product experience end to end.

I worked directly with:
- The CEO.
- Co-founders across product and research.

My responsibility spanned:
- Product UX.
- System and interaction design.
- Visual language and brand.
- Front-end design engineer implementation.

My focus was not on adding features, but on defining clear boundaries teams could build against.

---

# Key Decisions

1. Lead with brand to explain complexity
Instead of starting with technical controls, I leaned into a brand-driven approach to explain advanced concepts.
The product needed to feel intentional before users would trust it.

2. Design reliable flows for agents and collectives
Rather than exposing raw configuration, I focused on clear, repeatable flows for creating agents and collectives.
This made behavior predictable without removing flexibility.

3. Let internal clarity drive adoption
I optimized first for internal understanding and delight.
Once the system made sense to the team, external adoption followed naturally.

Several advanced capabilities were intentionally delayed to avoid overwhelming users before the core mental model was clear.

---

# Outcome

The product evolved from a static artifact into a governable system.

Teams could:
- Understand what agents were doing.
- Trust them with real assets.
- Compose and ship strategies with confidence.

This shift unlocked adoption not because agents became smarter, but because their behavior became legible.

---

# Reflection

This project reinforced something I've seen repeatedly in AI products:

Intelligence is rarely the bottleneck.
Trust, clarity, and governance are.

Designing for those early changes everything that comes after.
